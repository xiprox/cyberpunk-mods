## 2026-11-08: Shop colours reach parts outside the slots (compiled and generated, not installed, not seen)

A catalog only has a slot where a model has alternatives, so a whole-car paint missed the Legatus, MT28, BMF and Z71 bodies, the Bandit's separate shell and bumpers, the Porsche's trunk and more. `experiments/paint/list-parts.py` (first in the chain) reads each catalog's looks from their `.app` files and lists the exterior meshes that aren't slot options, leaving out by name what isn't bodywork (`NOT_BODY`: glass, lights, chassis, far-away stand-ins and so on); `extract.py` and `extract-geometry.py` take them in, and masks now come from every `vehicles\\.*\.mlmask$`, since a body mask needn't have "ext" in its name. `plan-paints.py` paints such a part only if a real body colour shows on it (not bare material, at least 15%); base paints are still chosen from the slots' parts. `shop-paints.yaml` gets `<catalog>.shopMeshes`, and `VCEngine.Apply` gives those components a whole-car `vcs_` colour (`Verify` checks them). A stock whole-car paint doesn't reach them, since nothing says they have it, and the paint tool can't aim at them yet, as they have no slot. Bare surfaces now include nylon, straps, fabric and the colour-checker test material.
- **Coverage:** about 620 meshes. Expected to stay incomplete: the Legatus (body, rear bumper, doors; not the hood, front bumper or trunk), Z71 Aras (nothing passes), Hozuki (paint held outside the mesh). Far-away stand-in meshes keep stock paint, so a car in the distance may show its old colour.
- **Not checked in the game:** all of this.

## 2026-11-08: Paint per corner (passes its test, 2026-11-08 evening)

One door can be painted without the others (UX.md, "The paint tool and the tool rack"). A build holds `corner=<slot>:<corner>:<paint>` fields (`VCBuild.cornerSlots`, `cornerNames`, `cornerPaints`); a slot with one part has the corner `all`. The order, top first: the corner's paint, the slot's, the whole car's where the part offers it, then the look's. A paint for the whole car leaves corners alone, as it leaves a part's own paint alone. A new part or a paint for the whole slot clears that slot's corners (`SetOption`, `ClearSlot`). `VCEngine.ChooseCornerPaint(vehicle, slot, corner, paint)`, no paint taking it back off; `Verify` checks each corner. Nothing in the menus sets it yet; the paint tool will.
- **Co-op protocol left at 3 for now:** a build with `corner=` fields only comes from the test until the paint tool exists. An older build ignores the field and would drop it on saving, the reason protocol 3 was raised for rims and tires, so raise it when the paint tool ships, with everyone updating together.
- **Test:** job `cornerpaint` on a Suburban Hella: one door, a second, the whole car under them, one taken off, the slot painted whole, with a picture each and every door's real appearance read from the car. Results in the paint tool's entry above.

## 2026-11-08: The shop's 89 colours on every model (generated, installed by the test run, most models right)

Built the way the probe proved: ArchiveXL dynamic materials (user's choice), chosen at build time. `experiments/paint/`, run in order with `python -I`: `extract.py` (the catalogs' meshes and the vehicles' paint files as WolvenKit JSON), `map-paints.py`, then WolvenKit `uncook` of `vehicles\\.*ext[^\\]*\.mlmask$` with `--uext png` into `cache/masks` and `shrink-masks.ps1`, then `plan-paints.py` and `make-paints.py`. `cache/` is 1.5 GB and out of git.
- **Which layer is the colour** (probe). What varies between a model's paints didn't answer it: layer 0 of most paint files is plastic or a surface that differs by paint. So it's measured: each layer's mask composited from the top down over UV space gives what each layer really shows on the part, and the body's colour is the layer showing most that isn't an overlay (dust, dirt, grime, rust, plaster, velvet, felt, carpet, tape and so on), with any other layer of the same surface and colour. Usually layer 0, sometimes 1 or 2, with a few others. UV area isn't area on the car, so this is a good estimate only.
- **What each model builds on:** the factory paint with the most body colour showing and the least dirt, among the looks most of its parts have; a part without that look uses its own cleanest. This can pick a livery (`growl_fm`, `netrunner_01`), whose graphics would stay on top of the new colour.
- **Numbers:** 47 model folders, 483 meshes, 147 of the game's paint files, 89 colours each (13,083 files), a patch per mesh; `vehiclecustomizer_paint.archive` 28 MB and its `.xl`; 775 catalog options marked. Appearances are `vcs_<colour>` (`vcs_alpine`, `vcs_hot_pink`). Interiors, seats, windows, tires, rims and kit add-ons get none.
- **The same colour on every car:** the body-colour layers are replaced whole with the Hella Suburban's layer 0 in our surface (`vehiclecustomizer\paint\vc_car_paint.mltemplate`, the game's metallic car paint with the 89 added), so tile, levels and microblend match. Every colour is metallic for now; finishes come with the paint tool.
- **Weak spots** (the colour shows on under 30% of the UV space, to check first): Riptide, Semimaru, Shion Nomad, Type-66, DeLeon, Apollo, Colby Pickup Nomad, Quartz Nomad.
- **Engine:** `VCData.Offers` accepts a `vcs_` name on an option marked `.shopPaint` in `shop-paints.yaml` (`Catalog.reds`, `Engine.reds`). Compiled. The menus and the paint tool don't offer these yet, so only the test can apply them.
- **Test:** job `shoppaint` (the sweep's third variant) puts every catalog's first style in `vcs_hot_pink` at noon, with a picture each, about 15 minutes with the game in front.
- **First run** (`dev/runs/2026-11-08_17-01-22_shoppaint`, 302 of 302 checks, pictures read by me on sheets, small and side on; the user watched part of it and is inclined to accept it for v1). All 483 patches loaded with no ArchiveXL error and no build failed. By eye, about 65 of the 100 catalogs are painted as they should be, trucks' cargo left alone. About 20 are partly painted: Quartz (cyan and gold panels left), Caliburn (nose only), Porsche (rear), Riptide, Apollo, the Nomad variants (Quartz, Shion, Type-66, Colby Pickup, Galena, Mackinaw), Merrimac `player_01`. About 14 show no colour: Bandit, Legatus, the five plain Zeyas, Hozuki, MT28 coach, Tanishi `player_01`, Mackinaw BMF, Z71 Aras, Apollo `ep1`, Kusanagi. Why (probe, report and game files): **no body in the catalog** (Legatus: a seat slot only; MT28: windows only; BMF and Z71: no slots), since a catalog only has slots where a model has alternatives and a whole-car paint only reaches slots; **paint not in the mesh** (Hozuki, Tanishi `player_01`: the body's materials name no paint file of their own, so it's presumably in the material instance they're based on); **mask name** (Kusanagi: `ml_v_sportbike1_yaiba_kusanagi_masksset`, without "ext", so `is_ext` skipped its body). **Bandit, the five plain Zeyas and Apollo `ep1`** report parts repainted and show no pink. Job `paintcheck` (`dev/runs/2026-11-08_17-39-33_paintcheck`) settled it: the patches are on those meshes (89 of ours in the game's own resource), the live parts really wear `vcs_hot_pink`, and each car looks exactly like the stock paint it was built from (`bandit_02`, `hazmat_01`), so the appearance is in use and the colour change isn't visible. Two causes. **Zeya: far chunks left out.** Its far material's mask is `yellow_masksset_lod`, with no "ext", so `is_ext` skipped it and the test's 12 m shows the far version in stock paint (the near chunk is painted; not yet seen up close). Ruled out: a missing rebuild (24 models built once took the colour). **Fixed in the generator, regenerated, not yet installed or seen:** `extract-geometry.py` exports every model folder's meshes as glb (1 minute), and `plan-paints.py` weighs each layer by the real triangles; a far material is body when it names the same paint file as a near body material; a mesh with no exterior-named mask takes any mask not named for something else (the Kusanagi, which gets one part of six); bare surfaces (iron, steel, aluminium, carbon, rubber, leather, chrome) only count if nothing else on the part shows; a paint file shared between models is now planned per model, since the Bandit and the Quartz use one file differently. ## 2026-11-08: The tools get their own weapon definition (installed at 16:26, not yet run)

The user asked for a clean definition instead of a Lexington copy: "overriding everything needed to make it a polished entry in inventory, etc. not scare citizens, no idle animations, sounds we can add later, no aim sway, no wrong hud, no melee". `Items.VC_ToolBase` (`tool.yaml`) is a new `WeaponItem` from nothing; `Items.VC_Tool` and `Items.VC_PaintTool` build on it. From the Lexington only the hold (`friendlyName`) and the rig and gun animations the `.app`s name remain, and the part records' `$base`, which hold no stats.
- **Left out on purpose** (from the game's tweak files): all damage, recoil, sway, spread, ricochet and RPG stat groups, weight, price, `audioName`, the `DriverCombat`, `DualWield`, `Handgun` and power-weapon tags, attacks, shooting patterns. A name and description of our own (`vc_tool_*`, a text file in the tools' archive, through ArchiveXL's `localization`; the wording is a placeholder for the user to change). `Quest` tag and `canDrop: false` stop drop and disassemble (`dropAction.script`, `disassembleAction.script`).
- **Kept, with the reason beside each in `tool.yaml`:** a trigger mode and `rangedAttacks` (`ReadyEvents` reads them every frame), a magazine of 1, since **the heavy slot drops a weapon whose magazine is empty** (`IsHeavyWeaponEmpty`), the Lexington's zoom stats for the wrench (`ZoomLevel`, `AimFOV`, `AimOffset`), short equip times, so no first-draw flourish.
- **`ToolRules.reds` (new), for both tools:** no quick melee (`CanQuickMelee`); no idle break (`ReadyEvents.OnTick`, keeping its timestamp fresh); citizens don't react (the game's `WeaponDisplayed` broadcast in `FirstEquipEvents` and `EquippedEvents`); no corner panel (`WeaponRosterGameController`). The firing block, raised stance, no-reload and goes-back-to-the-rack guards in `Tool.reds` now cover the paint tool too (`VCToolRules.IsAny`); `VCTool.IsTool` still means the wrench alone, for its menu.

## 2026-11-06: The build is a stack of layers over a style; the catalog is generated and every choice is an ID

Agreed with the user:

## 2026-11-05: Traps a part swap has to handle, and parts across styles

The user asked what small details like the wheel names we're missing. What the data shows (probe), most of it untested in the game:

- **Parts hang off other parts.** On V's Hella the mirrors and stripes hang off the doors, a trunk extra off the trunk, and `bumper_f_player_01` off the front bumper; on the 6th Street look the stickers hang off the hood, trunk and doors. Across all looks, thousands of parts hang off a hood, trunk, door or bumper. Swapping a part leaves what hangs off it where it was, fitted to the old shape; the piece lying on the ground in the third and fourth runs was probably V's bumper extra. A swap has to carry, swap or hide what hangs off the part.
- **One mount point, several parts.** Each wheel mount on the Hella holds a tire and a rim; each front door mount holds the door's stripe and mirror. So a build needs the mount point and the kind of part.
- **Shadows.** Panels and tires come with a separate shadow mesh, and a swap that leaves it keeps the old shape's shadow.
- **Paints belong to a mesh.** The junk bumper offers `color_02`, `color_03` and `burnt_01`, the stock bumper 22 others, so a paint carries over a swap only when the new mesh has one of that name.
- **Wrecks.** A destroyed car switches to its record's destroyed look (`vehicleComponent.script`), which drops the build until the car spawns again.
- **Wheel size.** The record's wheel setup sets the wheel radius, so a much bigger or smaller tire would float or sink unless the copy's wheel setup changes too.
- Still open from before: the stock model from far away, damage and parts breaking off, CrystalCoat recoloring cars that have its mask (the user runs CrystalCoat Continuity), and partners' copies of the car.

The user wants as much flexibility as possible, including parts that work across styles (user). The structure allows it: a style decides only which mount points exist, and every part the model uses at a mount point, in any of its looks, can go there.

## 2026-11-05: Copying a vehicle while playing is untried; if it fails, the question waits

The user asked whether a car can be copied in real time while customizing it, leaving the stock one stock.
They already know copies in a file work, as the [Racer copies](../nazare-racer-coop/r6/tweaks/nazareracercopies/nazareracercopies.yaml) do: Bleack rode the Shiro (record `0x29891e9bde`) and Bossun's game spawned its mirror from that record (logs).
If real-time copying doesn't work, this question is shelved for now (user).

On paper it takes three steps, which the test's "Copy this vehicle now" button runs on whatever you're in or looking at.
`TweakDB:CloneRecord` makes the record, the new id goes on `Vehicle.vehicle_list.list`, and `EnablePlayerVehicle` puts it in the garage.
Cloning from code at startup already runs on this PC, in `NightCoopPhotoSpiderRecords`, but that isn't mid-session.
The unknown is whether the game's vehicle system accepts a record added after it started; if not, the test's "Try unlocking them again" shows whether loading a save is enough.

A clone lasts only until the game closes, so the test makes each one again in `onTweak` from `live-copies.txt`, before a save that owns it loads.
What a save does with a garage entry whose record has gone is unknown, so the test belongs on a spare save.

Co-op is the harder half and isn't in this test.
NightCoop ["never asks the game to spawn a record it does not have"](../../vendor/nightcoop-sdk/docs/content-fallbacks.md) and gives a partner without it a stock stand-in, and it [opens a modded car in a guest's garage](../../vendor/nightcoop-sdk/docs/content-fallbacks.md) by a name it reads from `r6/tweaks/**.yaml`, where a live copy wouldn't be.
A record's id comes only from its name, so each machine could make the same one if told, and the SDK's shared state reaches late joiners, but whether that arrives before NightCoop needs the record is untried.

Part changes are meant to apply only to a vehicle whose record is a copy, so the stock one and traffic of that model keep their look.
A copy can also set `appearanceName`, giving it another of the car's looks with no script and with that look's own far-away model, which the test's second file copy tries.

Two players owning one copy may clash.
NightCoop treated guests' cars of one record as one car on build bacb705, and logs from 2026-11-04 still say "our own entity becomes its mirror" for scene cars and others of its kind 4.
The Shiro was kind 1 and got a spawned mirror, on a machine where it wasn't a player vehicle ("player vehicle here: false"), so that session didn't test it.
The [SDK docs](../../vendor/nightcoop-sdk/docs/shared-spawns.md) say the mirror is never the partner's own vehicle of the same record.

## 2026-11-05: Choices reach partners as entity state

The user confirmed that choices go through the NightCoop SDK.
[Entity state](../../vendor/nightcoop-sdk/docs/api.md) fits: a reliable value per key on a synced entity, up to 1,024 bytes, delivered to the partner's mirror, kept for a mirror that spawns later or is recreated, and sent to late joiners.
Only the machine leading the car can set it, and a value from anyone else is dropped.
When a partner drives the car the old values stay until that partner's machine sets its own, so a machine without the owner's choices must not write an empty list.
Part changes use only game files, so a partner needs the mod but no extra archive, unless we add new paints.

## 2026-11-05: Build it in game first; an outside app only for what the game can't preview

The game itself is the preview, while an outside app would have to reproduce the game's multilayer materials just to show a car.
An outside app earns its place for making new paints, or putting CrystalCoat on more cars, since those need generated files.
Both can share one saved format: a list of choices per copy.
If live changes fail in the test, the fallback is applying them as the car spawns (Codeware's `Entity/Assemble`), and after that generating car files as [Boe6's guide](https://wiki.redmodding.org/cyberpunk-2077-modding/modding-guides/vehicles/boe6s-guide-new-car-from-a-to-z/create-base-files) does.

Codeware adds `ChangeResource`, `ChangeAppearance`, `LoadAppearance` and `RefreshAppearance` to components, and `GetComponents` and `AddComponent` to entities.
[Its wiki](https://github.com/psiberx/cp2077-codeware/wiki) lists mesh, skinned, garment and morph components as supported.
Car parts are mostly `entPhysicalMeshComponent`, which the wiki doesn't list, though the game's scripts declare it `extends MeshComponent` (read in `tools/redmod/scripts`).
The [modding wiki's CrystalCoat guide](https://wiki.redmodding.org/cyberpunk-2077-modding/modding-guides/vehicles/crystalcoat-tm-1) changes part appearances from a script.

## 2026-11-05: Parts fit their own car; only wheels, tires and bolt-ons cross between cars

Bumper pivots differ per car: the Hella's front bumper spans X -0.00 to 1.98 m around its pivot, the Quartz Nomad's -0.29 to 0.98 m (probe).
Front bumpers range from 0.48 to 2.88 m wide, median 1.97 m, and only 11 of the possible pairs from different car names come within 3 cm in width and height, several of them variants of one car (probe).
Matching size wouldn't mean matching shape either, so bumpers, hoods and trunks stay with their own car.

Wheels and tires are different, because the game reuses them itself: the Galena Nomad's tire is on the Quartz, Shion, Type-66 and Colby, and the Porsche's wheel on the Quadra Sport, Type-66 and De Leon (probe).
The `v_common__nomad` kit is 14 bolt-on pieces used across 15 car families (probe).
Tires run from 0.47 to 1.62 m tall, so not every pair will look right.
Most Hella looks have a tire mesh and no wheel parts at all; only V's looks have four `wheel_01_*` parts, which is why the test uses that look (probe).

Per car family the typical choice is 3 front bumpers, 3 rear, 2 hoods and 4 trunks (probe).
These are candidates: a family includes variants such as Nomad, and whether their parts fit each other's bodies is unproven.
Each appearance also carries one baked whole-car model for distance (`entAppearanceProxyMeshComponent`), so a custom mix probably shows the stock look from far away.

## 2026-11-05: Traffic cars are mostly repainted, though parts are named and placed the same way

The idea began from traffic cars looking kit-bashed.
Across 126 appearance files and 1,452 looks (wrecks and decoration excluded), 6% of visible part slots ever change mesh between a car's looks, 38% only change paint, and the rest never change (probe).
A default Hella and a `suburban_1` Hella differ in paint on 15 parts and in their distance model, and in no part's shape (probe).
A body mesh has a median of 16.5 named paints, 38 at most, plus a default (probe).

Still, parts are built for mixing: a part is its own component with its own mesh and paint, attached to a named slot such as `bumper_front`, `hood_a` or `wheel_front_left` on `vehicle_slots`, and slot names mostly repeat across cars (probe).
V's own Hella is the game doing this: a junk rear bumper, a primer-black hood and trunk, Quadra Turbo wheels, and extras such as `bumper_f_player_01` (probe).
Body panels and tires have `_shadow` partners, which a swap may have to change too, though the game leaves the Hella's front bumper shadow alone when it fits the junk bumper (probe).

CrystalCoat's component is named in the shared `base_ground_4w` template, so most cars have it, but the wiki says each submesh also needs `CustomColorMask` in its material.
A search of body meshes finds that name on only 7 families, missing Herrera, Quadra and Villefort, which reports of patch 2.2 name as supported, so that list has to come from elsewhere.
